10 Genotype × pre-pregnancy BMI interactions

10.1 Executive summary

This report describes placenta genotype × maternal pre-pregnancy BMI (G×ppBMI) interaction mapping from Module 07 of the Placenta xQTL Atlas. It is a descriptive report of the supplied results, not a re-analysis of individual-level data.

The merged Tier-1 results contain 444,582 ancestry-specific feature tests for the exact exposure label ppBMI. At q <= r params$tier1_q, 2,067 rows are significant, representing 2,064 molecular features and approximately 1,449 Ensembl genes. Splicing accounts for 86.2% of significant Tier-1 rows, with a particularly strong contribution from EUR.

Cross-ancestry Tier-1 synthesis identifies 1,285 features with ACAT feature-level q <= r params$tier1_q. Only 2 features are significant in both ancestries, and the ancestry-specific lead variants for those features are not the same. Thus, the strongest cross-ancestry conclusion is evidence for recurrent G×ppBMI regulation of the same molecular phenotype, not direct replication of an identical variant-level interaction.

Tier 2 contains 35,090 prioritized phenotype-level results, of which 33,949 (96.7%) have q_meta <= r params$tier2_q. Because Tier 2 is a hypothesis-driven follow-up set and many rows are supported by only one ancestry, this near-saturated q-value yield should not be interpreted as 33,949 independent discoveries. A deliberately stricter sensitivity definition requiring two ancestries, concordant interaction direction, nominal P < r params$ancestry_nominal_p in both ancestries, and P_het >= r params$heterogeneity_p retains 664 features.

10.3 Analysis framework

For each molecular phenotype, Module 07 fits a cis interaction model of the form

Y = βGG + βEE + βG × E(G × E) + *Z**γ + ϵ*,

where E is standardized ppBMI, G is genotype dosage, and Z contains the optimized molecular-QTL covariates. The parameter of interest is βG × E: the change in the genotype effect per one-SD increase in ppBMI.

Tier 1 is ancestry-specific discovery with adaptive phenotype-level permutation and FDR control within ancestry × modality × exposure. Cross-ancestry synthesis is performed after ancestry-specific discovery. When ancestry lead variants differ, the repository deliberately labels the feature as discordant-lead and does not claim that the IVW statistic represents the same variant-level test.

Tier 2 is a hypothesis-driven scan of phenotypes prioritized from downstream genetic evidence (colocalization and significant isoTWAS/gene-level TWAS criteria in the current implementation). It performs nominal cis interaction testing within each ancestry, then variant-level IVW meta-analysis where possible, retains the best meta-analysis variant for each prioritized phenotype, and applies BH correction across the prioritized set.

A notable QC feature is that 33,949 of 35,090 prioritized phenotypes have q_meta <= r params$tier2_q. This very high yield should be interpreted cautiously:

  1. the set is genetically pre-prioritized rather than genome-wide;
  2. most Tier-2 phenotype rows have support from only one ancestry; and
  3. the finalizer computes a normal-theory IVW z-test from βG × E/*S**E*, whereas the ancestry-level nominal interaction p-values are based on the within-ancestry regression test. For one-ancestry rows these need not be numerically identical, especially with finite degrees of freedom.

For these reasons, this report emphasizes the two-ancestry sensitivity subset below rather than equating all Tier-2 q-values with independent replicated discoveries.

10.9 Interpretation guardrails

  1. An interaction is not a ppBMI main effect. The reported coefficient asks whether the genotype–molecular-phenotype relationship changes across ppBMI.
  2. Ancestry-specific lead variants are not interchangeable. A phenotype significant in EAS and EUR can still have different lead variants, so opposite signs at discordant leads do not establish effect reversal of the same allele.
  3. Tier 1 and Tier 2 answer different questions. Tier 1 is discovery; Tier 2 is genetically prioritized follow-up and is therefore enriched by construction.
  4. The Tier-2 q-value distribution is unusually saturated. Two-ancestry concordance, nominal ancestry-level evidence, and heterogeneity are useful reporting diagnostics.
  5. Modality counts are correlated. Multiple splice junctions, isoforms, or 3’/5’ events from the same gene are not independent biological discoveries.
  6. Effect magnitudes are scale-dependent. ppBMI and molecular phenotypes are standardized according to the Module-07 workflow, so coefficients should be interpreted in that standardized context.
  7. No causal claim follows from G×E alone. These results identify ppBMI-dependent genetic regulation; causal mediation of pregnancy outcomes requires additional evidence.

10.11 Conclusions

The ppBMI G×E atlas provides strong evidence that placental cis-regulatory effects can vary across the maternal pre-pregnancy BMI spectrum. The principal descriptive features are:

  • a large Tier-1 interaction set with pronounced ancestry and modality heterogeneity;
  • a dominant splicing contribution, especially in EUR;
  • very limited direct feature-level significance in both ancestries and widespread discordance of ancestry-specific lead variants;
  • a large, enriched Tier-2 follow-up set whose near-saturated q-value yield warrants stricter two-ancestry sensitivity summaries; and
  • a smaller subset of recurrent genes and molecular phenotypes supported by multiple internal G×ppBMI evidence dimensions.

The most defensible next prioritization step is to combine these G×ppBMI signals with the independent fine-mapping, colocalization/colocBoost, TWAS, and cell-type attribution evidence generated elsewhere in the Placenta xQTL Atlas.
