1 Multi-ancestry placental xQTL analysis

1.1 Executive summary

This report summarizes the multi-ancestry placental xQTL analysis: cohort-only genotype PCA on LD-pruned variants within each ancestry stratum, a conventional covariate set (first 5 genotype PCs + HCP factors + sex, maternal age, and cell-type fractions; maternal cell fraction excluded before correlation pruning at |r| > 0.9; HCP count optimized per ancestry × modality via module 25b; no covariate cap), a MAF ≥ 0.01 filter with a ≥ 5 minor-allele-count floor at the genotype intersection, Storey q-values (computed with the R qvalue package), and per-phenotype normalization of all modalities (TMM → variance-stabilizing transformation for the two count-based expression modalities; rank-based inverse-normal transformation for the ratio-based modalities; isoform usage mapped as within-gene relative-abundance ratios). Mapping covers 36 ancestry × modality × layer cells (EAS n ≈ 281, EUR n ≈ 136). The results narrative follows the figure progression of the developing-brain xQTL atlas (Wen et al., Science 2024): cohort landscape → calibration → the modality-wise regulation landscape → cross-ancestry regulation → the discovery catalog → functional characterization.

Headline result: 8116 phenotype-level associations reach FDR ≤ 5% in the discovery layer (ungrouped scan for the 8 multi-phenotype modalities + the per-gene expression scan): 3606 in EAS and 4510 in EUR, implicating 1757 / 2243 unique genes respectively. This phenotype-level set is the input to cross-ancestry SuSHiE fine-mapping (Methods). At gene level, the combined cross-modality catalog contains 1404 EAS and 1654 EUR eGenes at FDR ≤ 5%, with per-modality grouped eGenes as the breakdown. Strongest association per ancestry: EAS: PGM1 (expression, grouped layer, q = 5.4e-29); EUR: ERAP1 (alt_polyA, ungrouped layer, q = 1.8e-35). Storey calibration estimates a signal fraction (π1) of 0–34% across cells. In high-signal cells Storey q-values are correspondingly more liberal than Benjamini–Hochberg (by up to the estimated π0); the per-cell comparison is in Calibration.

The splicing modality splits by ancestry. At its modality-matched HCP count (HCP count optimization), EUR splicing yields 35 FDR ≤ 5% eGenes — under expression-derived covariates the layer carried no detectable signal, so the EUR splicing null was a covariate-specification artifact, now resolved. EAS splicing remains near-null in both the grouped (5 eGenes, best q = 0.0021) and ungrouped (5 phenotype-level hits) layers, its eGene yield is flat across the HCP grid, and its p-values depart only mildly from the null at the median (genomic-control λ = 1.51; Calibration). Three lines of evidence bear on the residual EAS null:

  1. The machinery detects splicing signal where it exists — the same pipeline and covariate machinery yield the EUR splicing catalog above at n ≈ 136, a higher detection floor (r² ≈ 0.19) than EAS (r² ≈ 0.10 at n = 281; Power analysis). A pure sample-power explanation is therefore insufficient for EAS: EUR splicing effects cleared a higher bar. The leading candidate is EAS-specific attenuation of the splicing phenotypes — EAS pools four cohorts spanning single- and paired-end protocols, and junction-level quantification is the most protocol-sensitive of the nine modalities.
  2. The EAS null is not a covariate artifact — eGene yield does not improve at any HCP count on the grid (HCP count optimization), and the mild median λ elevation with only a handful of FDR-significant hits is the signature of diffuse, individually weak effects rather than of residual confounding (which would inflate the median without bound) or of a dead layer (λ ≈ 1, no tail). The FDR-significant hits in the other modalities carry estimated r² of 0.00–3.59 — exactly the class of outsize effect the floor permits.
  3. Signal replicates across ancestries — cross-ancestry rank concordance of gene-level signal on the combined layer is Spearman ρ = 0.18 (top-100 overlap 31/100), with same-variant slope concordance 0.54 — the pattern expected of real, partially shared genetic regulation, not of artifact-driven signal.

Carrier-count audit: clean. The MAC ≥ 5 floor (applied at the genotype intersection in module 23) propagated to every mapping output — minimum minor-allele carrier count across all tested pairs is 27 (EAS) and 13 (EUR). # Methods

1.1.1 Study cohorts and data

Placental RNA-seq and genotype data were assembled from four cohorts (Table 1). NIEHS_RICHS contributed placenta RNA-seq and array genotypes under dbGaP controlled access. GUSTO contributed placenta RNA-seq and array genotypes through the study investigators. SNUH contributed placenta RNA-seq and genotypes available under SRA BioProject PRJNA820329 (102 RNA-seq runs). NIGMS contributed placenta RNA-seq and whole-exome sequencing (WXS) under SRA BioProject PRJNA671171 (154 RNA-seq runs and 95 WXS runs); each NIGMS subject was sampled at two placental sites, and the replicate libraries were averaged per individual before mapping. All genotype and transcriptomic data were processed against the GRCh38 no-alt analysis set (GCA_000001405.15).

1.1.2 Genotype processing and imputation

Array genotypes were lifted over to GRCh38 where required. For NIGMS, WXS reads were retrieved from SRA, aligned to GRCh38 with BWA, processed to per-sample gVCFs with GATK, and jointly genotyped per chromosome. Each cohort was filtered before imputation to biallelic SNPs with per-variant missingness of at most 0.05, minor allele frequency (MAF) of at least 0.01, and Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (HWE) test P above 1 x 10^-6, and variants were left-aligned and normalized against the reference genome with bcftools norm. Genotypes were imputed on either the TOPMed (NIGMS, NIEHS_RICHS) or SEAD (GUSTO, SNUH) imputation servers. Imputed variants with imputation Rsq of at least 0.4 were retained and converted to plink2 binary format (pgen).

1.1.3 Ancestry assignment and cross-cohort genotype pooling

Genetic ancestry was assigned within each cohort with PC-AiR as implemented in GENESIS, using kinship estimated with KING in SNPRelate and 1000 Genomes phase 3 samples as the unrelated reference set. Each sample was assigned to one of five continental ancestry strata (AFR, AMR, EAS, EUR, SAS). For each stratum, the Rsq-passing variants of each cohort were subset to the assigned samples, multiallelic sites were split per cohort so that imputed dosages were partitioned correctly, and cohort VCFs were merged across cohorts with bcftools merge and normalized against GRCh38. Pooled genotypes were filtered to MAF of at least 0.01, HWE P above 1 x 10^-6, and per-variant missingness of at most 0.05, and palindromic duplicate sites were removed. The resulting per-ancestry pgen files define the genotype identifier space used by all downstream steps.

1.1.4 RNA-seq processing

RNA-seq reads for SNUH and NIGMS were retrieved from SRA with prefetch and fasterq-dump; GUSTO and NIEHS_RICHS FASTQs were already local. Adapters and low-quality bases were trimmed with Trim Galore 0.6.10. Reads were aligned to GRCh38 with STAR 2.7.4a in basic two-pass mode. Transcript abundance was quantified with Salmon 1.10.2 against the HPLRv2 long-read placental transcriptome annotation using automatic library-type detection, selective alignment with validateMappings, and 20 bootstrap replicates per sample.

1.1.5 Transcriptomic phenotyping

Nine RNA modalities were quantified per sample with an in-house rewrite of the PANTRY phenotyping pipeline (github.com/PejLab/Pantry) adapted to the local compute cluster. Gene expression was quantified from Salmon with tximport, both as gene-level TPM (used for detection filtering) and as gene-level lengthScaledTPM count-scaled abundances (tximport countsFromAbundance = “lengthScaledTPM”), the latter serving as the TMM-VST normalization input. Isoform expression was taken as transcript-level TPM after quantification uncertainty correction (see below), with TPM recomputed from the QU-corrected counts and Salmon effective lengths; the QU-corrected transcript counts themselves served as the TMM-VST normalization input for isoform expression. Isoform usage was computed as within-gene relative abundance ratios after correcting transcript counts for quantification uncertainty: edgeR catchSalmon was used to estimate per-transcript overdispersion from the Salmon bootstrap replicates, and counts were divided by the estimated overdispersion (Chen et al., Nucleic Acids Res 2023). Alternative transcription start site and alternative polyadenylation usage were quantified with txrevise: event annotations were constructed in two event groups, each split into upstream, contained, and downstream positional classes, and event abundance was quantified with Salmon against the six resulting transcript indices. Splice junction usage was quantified by extracting junctions from the STAR alignments with RegTools and clustering them with leafCutter (minimum 30 reads per cluster, minimum cluster ratio 0.001); intron usage ratios within clusters were carried as phenotypes. Intron retention was quantified with MAJIQ. RNA editing levels were computed at REDIportal sites (TABLE1_hg38_v3) from samtools mpileup output as the proportion of edited reads per site. RNA stability was approximated per gene as the ratio of exonic to intronic read counts from featureCounts. Because splice junctions and retained introns are defined by the observed data in each cohort, splicing and intron retention features were harmonized across cohorts by stable genomic coordinates before pooling. Phenotypes were exported as bgzipped, tabix-indexed BED files with gene-level phenotype groupings where applicable.

1.1.6 Cell-type deconvolution

Cell-type proportions were estimated from bulk gene expression with MuSiC, using a published placental single-cell RNA-seq reference (GSE182381). The estimated fractions were carried into the mapping covariates.

1.1.7 RNA to DNA sample linkage

RNA-seq sample identifiers were linked to genotype sample identifiers within each cohort using cohort-specific keys: SRA run-to-subject metadata for SNUH and NIGMS, and array subject identifiers for NIEHS_RICHS and GUSTO. The linkage table was checked for match rates, duplicates, and missing values before mapping.

1.1.8 Phenotype pooling and normalization

Within each ancestry stratum, per-cohort phenotype matrices were pooled and normalized in a single pass. The two count-based expression modalities (gene expression and isoform expression) followed the PsychENCODE/isoTWAS convention (Bhattacharya et al., Nat Genet 2023): after the detection filter, pooled count matrices — gene-level lengthScaledTPM counts from tximport for gene expression, and QU-corrected transcript counts for isoform expression — were normalized with edgeR TMM followed by the DESeq2 variance-stabilizing transformation, with TMM effective library sizes supplied to DESeq2 as size factors (counts themselves never pre-divided), and no subsequent rank transformation. The six ratio-based modalities followed the conventions of the developing-brain xQTL atlas (Wen et al., Science 2024): filtered matrices were quantile normalized across the pooled stratum, and each phenotype was then rank inverse-normal transformed separately within each cohort, so every cohort enters mapping on an identical standard-normal scale. Gene and isoform expression were filtered to features with TPM above 0.1 in more than 25% of samples (the filter is computed on the TPM matrices and the retained feature set is carried onto the count matrices), and ratio-based modalities were filtered to features detected in at least 40% of samples. For expression, sample outliers were removed from the normalized matrix using signed bicor network connectivity (z below -3). Cohort effects are absorbed by cohort indicator covariates in the mapping model — and, for the VST-normalized expression modalities, by cohort indicators included as known covariates during hidden-factor estimation — rather than by adjusting the phenotype matrix.

1.1.9 Genotype principal components and sample quality control

Within each stratum, pooled genotypes were LD-pruned (plink2 indep-pairwise 200 50 0.2) and 20 principal components (PCs) were computed exactly; the first five PCs were retained as covariates. Genotype and phenotype sample sets were intersected on the linkage map, and variants were further required to have a minor allele count of at least 5 in the intersected samples. Samples more than 6 standard deviations from the mean on any of the first five genotype PCs were excluded.

1.1.10 Covariate assembly and hidden factor estimation

Mapping covariates comprised the first five genotype PCs, sex, maternal age, estimated cell-type fractions (with the dominant cell type as the compositional reference and the maternal fraction excluded), cohort indicator covariates, and hidden confounding factors estimated from the normalized expression matrix with Rhcpp, using Picard alignment quality-control metrics and cohort indicators as known covariates. Covariates with near-zero variance were dropped, and the remaining set was pruned iteratively at pairwise absolute correlation above 0.9, with retention priority given to sex and maternal age, then genotype PCs, then cell types, then hidden factors. The number of hidden factors was optimized separately for each ancestry and modality: factors were re-estimated at each k in {0…5…100} by 5, chromosome 1 was mapped with tensorQTL under each candidate covariate set (the full genome was mapped for modalities with fewer than 300 chromosome-1 phenotypes), and the k yielding the most eGenes at Storey q of at most 0.05 was selected, with ties resolved to the smaller k.

1.1.11 cis-xQTL mapping

cis-QTL mapping was performed per ancestry stratum and modality with tensorQTL 1.0.10 under GTEx conventions: a cis window of 1 Mb on either side of the phenotype start, MAF of at least 0.01, nominal and permutation passes, and Storey q-values computed on the permutation beta-approximated P values with the R qvalue package. For modalities with gene-level phenotype groupings, grouped permutation (group_s) was used to select one lead variant per gene; the remaining modalities were mapped ungrouped. Multiple independent signals per gene (grouped layers) or per phenotype (ungrouped layer) were identified with the stepwise conditional mode (map_independent). Three result layers were produced per ancestry: grouped, ungrouped, and a combined cross-modality layer in which phenotype identifiers are namespaced by modality.

1.1.12 Cross-ancestry fine-mapping

FDR-significant lead phenotypes (q of at most 0.05) were pooled across ancestries per modality — from the primary per-gene scan for expression and from the ungrouped phenotype-level scan for all other modalities — and each locus was fine-mapped jointly across ancestries with SuSHiE (version 0.20 or later) in individual-level mode, using in-sample linkage disequilibrium from the intersected genotypes and the exact tested cis windows. The number of effects per locus was set to min(10, max(5, n_independent + 2)), where n_independent is the number of stepwise-conditional signals at the locus, and credible sets were filtered at a purity threshold of 0.5. Per-variant posterior inclusion probabilities (PIPs), credible-set membership, per-ancestry effect weights, and the cross-ancestry effect-size correlation were recorded for each locus.

1.1.13 Functional enrichment of fine-mapped variants

High-confidence variants (PIP of at least 0.9) were compared against all fine-mapped variants for enrichment in functional annotations. Variant consequences were annotated with fastVEP (GRCh38) and grouped into splice, loss-of-function, missense, synonymous, UTR, intron, regulatory, flanking, and intergenic classes. Regulatory annotations comprised ENCODE SCREEN candidate cis-regulatory element classes and placenta open-chromatin regions. Enrichment was tested with Fisher exact tests as the primary analysis, logistic regression adjusting for log10 distance to the transcription start site as a sensitivity analysis, and PIP-weighted enrichment; Benjamini-Hochberg FDR was applied within each test family.

1.1.14 Report-integration targeted lookups

To support cross-ancestry and cross-modality report panels without shipping genome-wide summary statistics, targeted association lookups were performed (script 35): for each FDR-significant lead (variant, phenotype) pair, the same pair’s slope, standard error, and nominal P value were recomputed in the other ancestry stratum and, for grouped-layer leads, across the gene’s phenotypes in every other modality within the same ancestry, by ordinary least squares on covariate-residualized genotypes and phenotypes. All unique lead variants were annotated with fastVEP consequence classes and intersected with ENCODE cCRE classes and placenta open-chromatin regions, and per-gene loss-of-function constraint metrics (pLI, LOEUF) were taken from gnomAD v2.1.1. Regional association scans (plus-minus 1 Mb) with linkage disequilibrium (r^2) to the lead variant were computed for two showcase loci (ERAP2 and the top shared non-expression-driver locus).

1.1.15 Software and computing

1.2 Pipeline and calibration

Processing pipeline and mapping calibration, in the style of DevBrain Fig. S2 (pipeline) and Figs. S3/S5/S6 (per-modality mapping QC: HCP optimization, permutation calibration, signal fraction).

1.2.1 Pipeline recap

  • Phenotypes: 9 RNA modalities (gene expression, isoform usage, isoform expression, splice junction usage, intron retention, alternative TSS, alternative polyA, RNA editing, RNA stability), harmonized to genotyped samples (array_id), with NIGMS technical replicates averaged per individual. The two isoform modalities are both built from QU-corrected Salmon transcript counts (mean count ≥ 10), length-normalized to TPM (count / effective length, scaled per sample to sum to 10^6): isoform usage is within-gene relative-abundance ratios (transcript TPM / gene-total TPM; PANTRY convention, mean ratio in \[0.05, 0.95\]) and isoform expression is the pre-ratio transcript TPM abundance (DevBrain §3.3 convention). Per ancestry stratum: feature filtering (isoform usage: usage > 0.1 in > 25% of samples; isoform expression: TPM > 0.1 in > 25% of samples, DevBrain §3.3; ratio modalities: detected in ≥ 40%, DevBrain §3.4). The two count-based expression modalities (gene expression, isoform expression) are then normalized by edgeR TMM -> DESeq2 VST on the pooled count matrices (gene-level lengthScaledTPM counts; QU-corrected transcript counts), terminal — no rank-based INT — with cohort indicators as known covariates in HCP estimation and in the mapping model (PsychENCODE/isoTWAS convention). The ratio-based modalities are batch-median NA-imputed, quantile-normalized across the pooled stratum + rank-based inverse-normal transformed per phenotype within each cohort, with cohort indicator covariates in the mapping model absorbing residual cohort effects. For the INT modalities no log2 pre-transform is applied: rank-based INT is invariant to any monotone transform, so log2/VST would be exact no-ops on the mapped scale.
  • Genotypes: ancestry-stratified plink2 pgen; variants filtered to MAF ≥ 0.01 with MAC ≥ 5 (≥ 5 minor alleles) at the genotype intersection — the count floor excludes low-carrier tests, which can produce spurious associations, while MAF 0.01 keeps the variant pool as large as defensible. tensorQTL’s own MAF ≥ 0.01 filter applies on top at mapping time.
  • Genotype PCA: cohort-only PCA per ancestry on LD-pruned variants (plink2 --indep-pairwise 200 50 0.2, then --pca 20 exact) — the GTEx convention.
  • Covariates: per ancestry × modality, first-5 genotype PCs + HCP factors 1–k* (k* selected per ancestry × modality by module 25b, chr1 eGene maximization on each modality’s own HCPs) + sex + maternal age + cell-type fractions (Syncytiotrophoblast as reference). The maternal cell-fraction covariate (ct_Maternal) is excluded BEFORE pruning by analysis decision (coded --exclude-covariates); the remaining pool is correlation-pruned (iterative max-|r| > 0.9 removal, protecting sex > PCs > cell types > HCPs — correlated HCPs are dropped, so effective k can fall below nominal k*) with no covariate cap (details and QC below).
  • Mapping (tensorQTL, cis window ±1 Mb): (i) per-modality grouped — all phenotypes of a gene within a modality tested as one group (permutation-calibrated gene-level p-value; the row label is the driver phenotype); (ii) combined — all phenotypes of a gene across all modalities as one cross-modality group (PANTRY’s headline design); \(iii\) ungrouped — per-phenotype lead variants for transcript-level resolution, and the discovery layer of record for fine-mapping; \(iv\) stepwise (cis.map_independent) for conditionally independent signals, entered at FDR ≤ 5% and run on the grouped, ungrouped, and combined layers alike.
  • Multiple testing: Storey q-values on the permutation-calibrated p-value (pval_beta), computed with the R qvalue package (default lambda) — the GTEx convention, and statistically identical to tensorQTL’s own calculate_qvalues. (On this cluster the rpy2/conda-R route to qvalue hits an unresolvable libstdc++/GLIBCXX conflict, so q-values are computed through a file-based bridge to the singularity R container used elsewhere in the pipeline.)

1.6 Functional enrichment

This section replaces the Biomni-era enrichment caches with a reproducible analysis built from the same archive used by the rest of this report. On the first render for an archive — or whenever the archive’s ungrouped top scan tables, gene_map.tsv, or the static pregnancy-set annotation are newer than the cached enrichment output — the report rebuilds the per-modality ORA locally. The derived enrichment files are written under the archive-specific subdirectory of params$derived_dir; they are never read from inputs_freeze/xqtl_enrichment_report_files.

Two complementary analyses are shown:

  1. Per-modality ORA — genes carrying at least one significant cis-xQTL (q < 0.05) in the ungrouped top-variant scan, separately for 8 multi-phenotype modalities x 2 ancestries. GO BP/MF/CC, KEGG, MSigDB Hallmark, and a curated pregnancy/placenta collection are tested under two universes: the HPLRv2 reference annotation and the genes tested in the corresponding ancestry-modality stratum.
  2. Combined-catalog ORA — combined-layer eGene sets (per ancestry, shared, and ancestry-specific) are queried with g:Profiler against the matching tested-gene background. This remains an optional live-service analysis and is skipped cleanly when g:Profiler is unavailable.

1.6.3 Enrichment limitations

  1. Large discovery sets dilute ORA specificity. When a large fraction of the tested universe is significant, only the strongest deviations from the background remain detectable; absence of enrichment does not imply absence of pathway biology.
  2. Annotation visibility is incomplete. HPLRv2-novel genes and Ensembl genes absent from a database’s identifier map can contribute to xQTL discovery but not to that database’s ORA.
  3. Pathway redundancy matters. Multiple significant terms can encode the same underlying gene cluster (especially HLA/immune and ribosomal terms), so term counts are not counts of independent biological mechanisms.
  4. Small curated sets have limited power. Pregnancy-set results should be interpreted from effect/overlap sizes as well as FDR.
  5. ORA is not causal. Enrichment can prioritize biology but cannot establish regulatory mediation of a pregnancy trait; colocalization and functional follow-up are required.
  6. Remote resources are snapshots. KEGG, MSigDB retrieval, and g:Profiler can change with database updates or be temporarily unavailable. The derived enrichment directory records the databases that were successfully built.

1.7 Biological interpretation

The discovery landscape: 8116 FDR ≤ 5% phenotype-level associations (3606 EAS / 4510 EUR, ungrouped + expression layers) implicating 1757 / 2243 unique genes, with the gene-level combined-layer catalog at 1404 EAS and 1654 EUR eGenes (n ≈ 281/136, MAF ≥ 0.01 + MAC ≥ 5, genome-wide permutation calibration). Strongest per ancestry: EAS: PGM1 (expression, grouped layer, q = 5.4e-29, r² ≈ 0.06); EUR: ERAP1 (alt_polyA, ungrouped layer, q = 1.8e-35, r² ≈ 0.59). Hit effect sizes (r² 0.00–3.59) sit in the class the power floor permits. All hits should be treated as candidates: visual QC of phenotype levels by genotype and replication in larger placental cohorts come before functional claims.

Three characterization observations:

  1. Modality composition. expression leads the EAS grouped-eGene breakdown (1123 of 2889); expression leads the EUR grouped-eGene breakdown (1528 of 3627) PANTRY’s central finding was that non-expression modalities carry the largest share of regulatory signal; the driver-modality composition of the catalog (above) tests whether that ordering holds in placenta.
  2. Ancestry sharing. 572 of 1403 EAS and 1650 EUR combined-layer eGenes are shared (Fisher OR 6.44, p = 1.8e-173 vs the tested-in-both universe). Shared eGenes are the cross-ancestry replication signal; specific sets are candidates for ancestry-biased regulation.
  3. Functional enrichment. Top g:Profiler term per set: EAS combined eGenes — Metabolic pathways (KEGG, g:SCS p = 8.6e-08); EAS-specific eGenes — Metabolic pathways (KEGG, g:SCS p = 0.003); EUR combined eGenes — Glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism (KEGG, g:SCS p = 0.035); shared eGenes — Metabolic pathways (KEGG, g:SCS p = 0.0017). 1.7.1 Literature context

Modality-matched covariates, in line with QTL practice. Latent-factor covariates are a standard power lever in QTL mapping (Stegle et al. 2012), and splicing layers — quantified annotation-free from intron usage (Li et al. 2017) — have hidden structure that need not track gene expression. The per-modality HCP choice behaved as that practice predicts: EUR splicing was null under expression-derived factors and recovers 35 eGenes at its own modality-matched k*, while the EAS splicing null is flat across the entire k grid, excluding covariate specification as its cause.

Sub-threshold top-ranked genes (tables above) remain prioritization hints only.

1.8 Limitations and next steps

  1. Power. n ≈ 281/136 with a ~12k-gene testing burden detects only r² ≳ 0.10 (EAS) / 0.19 (EUR) effects. Doubling n would roughly halve the detectable r²; matching PANTRY’s n = 445 would bring the floor to ≈ 0.06. Adding the remaining cohorts and the AFR/AMR/SAS strata is the highest-value next step.
  2. EAS splicing is near-null. The layer yields a handful of FDR ≤ 5% eGenes while EUR splicing — at a higher detection floor — yields 35. The EAS result is robust to HCP count (flat k-grid), so covariate specification is excluded as the cause; the leading candidate is cross-cohort heterogeneity in junction-level quantification (EAS pools four cohorts spanning single- and paired-end protocols). Revisiting splicing quantification harmonization across the EAS cohorts, and adding EAS cohorts, are the targeted next steps.
  3. Technical replicates. 25 mapped individuals (4 EAS, 21 EUR) have two RNA-seq runs. Phenotype values come from the single normalized run per individual, while run-level covariates (cell-type fractions, QC metrics) are averaged across the pair — a slight phenotype/covariate misalignment for those individuals. Future runs will collapse replicates at the count level before normalization so phenotypes and covariates are averaged consistently, with a pre-collapse replicate-concordance QC gate.
  4. Carrier-count propagation. The MAC ≥ 5 floor propagated cleanly to every mapping output (audit table above).
  5. Ancestry asymmetry. EUR tests more phenotypes than EAS in every modality (ancestry-specific filtering), so cross-ancestry comparisons are restricted to genes tested in both.
  6. No pooled cross-ancestry inference was performed. If combined discovery is wanted later, Fisher/Stouffer or an inverse-variance slope meta-analysis are the clean options.
  7. Stepwise secondary signals: 964 units carry a conditionally independent secondary signal (table above).
  8. Replication path: the FDR-significant hits are the priorities for lookup in larger placental resources or a future meta-analysis; visual QC of phenotype levels by genotype is the immediate sanity check for each.
