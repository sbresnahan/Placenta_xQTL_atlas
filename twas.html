7 Transcriptome-wide association study

7.1 Executive summary

This report summarizes placental transcriptome-wide association testing performed with FUSION prediction weights at both gene and isoform resolution. The supplied aggregate contains 258,124 model-level tests representing 8,075 primary Ensembl genes across 16 GWAS traits, together with 131,523 gene-level ACAT aggregation rows.

Headline result. At the supplied false-discovery threshold, 3,041 model-level associations have TWAS.Q < 0.05, and 2,098 gene × trait × weight-set rows have acat_q < 0.05. The gene-level set corresponds to 2,032 distinct gene–trait combinations before collapsing weight sets.

Among significant TWAS gene–trait pairs, 31 are also represented by a called SuSiE-coloc gene–trait pair and 12 by a mixed xQTL–GWAS colocBoost event; 40 have support from at least one colocalization framework.

The recurrent TWAS landscape contains both well-established body-weight genes—most prominently ADCY3, LEP, and LEPR—and placenta-centered candidates such as ELP5, CLDN7, CTDNEP1, SLC38A1, FAP, PLCE1-AS1, and SH3YL1. TWAS should be interpreted as evidence that the genetically predicted placental molecular phenotype is associated with the GWAS trait; it is not by itself proof of mediation or of a unique causal gene.

7.2 Methods

7.2.1 FUSION model-level tests

FUSION predicts the genetically regulated component of a molecular phenotype from cis-genetic weights and tests that predicted component against GWAS summary statistics. Here the ID field includes both gene models and long-read isoform models. A model is classified as isoform-level when its identifier contains __; otherwise it is classified as a gene-level model.

The report treats the supplied TWAS.P and TWAS.Q values as authoritative. Because a small number of extremely significant p-values are stored as numeric zero, all plotting code floors p-values at .Machine$double.xmin before -log10 transformation; the underlying result tables are not altered.

7.2.2 Gene-level ACAT aggregation

twas_gene_results.tsv.gz combines the available gene and isoform model p-values for each gene × trait × weight-set cell with ACAT. acat_q is used for headline gene-level discovery. The ACAT statistic is direction-free; when this report displays a direction it explicitly labels it as the lead model TWAS Z, not a gene-level ACAT effect direction.

7.2.3 Prediction weight sets

weight_set denotes the placental prediction-weight panel (EAS, EUR, or pooled). It should not be read as a balanced replication design. The external GWAS resources available to each ancestry differ substantially, so raw EAS-versus-EUR hit counts are not directly comparable. Pooled weights provide the broadest trait coverage.

7.6 Gene versus isoform signal

Because multiple gene and isoform models can represent the same primary gene and are correlated through local LD, raw model counts are not independent biological discoveries. The gene-level ACAT layer is therefore the preferred primary summary; model-level results are most useful for identifying which transcript model drives a gene-level association.

7.8 Integration with colocalization

TWAS and colocalization answer different questions. TWAS tests association of a genetically predicted placental molecular phenotype with a GWAS trait. SuSiE-coloc asks whether the xQTL and GWAS are consistent with a shared causal signal. colocBoost jointly searches for multi-trait colocalization events. Convergence therefore raises priority, while lack of colocalization does not automatically invalidate a TWAS association.

7.11 Literature context

The companion literature_twas.md reviews the TWAS architecture and the strongest convergent loci in the context of previous placental transcriptomics, placental multi-omics, nutrient transport, and obesity genetics. It also distinguishes three evidence tiers useful for manuscript writing:

  1. TWAS + colocalization + prior placental evidence (highest mechanistic priority);
  2. TWAS + colocalization with limited prior placental literature (novel atlas-driven candidates);
  3. recurrent TWAS without colocalization (association candidates requiring stronger causal disambiguation).

7.12 Interpretation and limitations

  1. TWAS association is not mediation. A significant result can arise from shared LD or horizontally pleiotropic genetic effects and does not prove that the placental molecular phenotype mediates the GWAS association.
  2. ACAT is direction-free. Gene-level ACAT combines model p-values; any displayed TWAS Z belongs to the lead component model, not to the ACAT aggregate itself.
  3. Gene and isoform models are correlated. Multiple significant transcript models from one locus should not be counted as independent biological mechanisms.
  4. Prediction quality varies. MODELCV.R2 should accompany interpretation of individual models, particularly ancestry-specific models with smaller training sets.
  5. Weight-set coverage is unbalanced across GWAS resources. EAS/EUR discovery counts are not a formal replication comparison.
  6. Very small p-values underflow to zero. This affects display precision but not the conclusion that these are extremely significant associations.
  7. Colocalization is complementary. TWAS can be significant without a called coloc event and vice versa; each method has distinct power and modeling assumptions.
  8. Placental specificity is not established by this report. Some recurrent genes (for example ADCY3, LEP, LEPR) have important biology in other tissues; tissue-specific causal claims require external comparison.
  9. GWAS trait semantics matter. Maternal and fetal birth-weight effects, gestational duration, and childhood body-size/BMI phenotypes have distinct causal interpretations.
