5 GWAS colocalization

5.1 Executive summary

This report describes cross-ancestry colocalization between placental cis-xQTL signals and GWAS traits spanning birth weight, gestational duration, and childhood anthropometry. Colocalization is performed in a multiple-causal-signal framework: SuSiE decomposes each xQTL and GWAS locus into credible-set signals, and coloc evaluates each signal pair.

The supplied aggregate contains 39,822 diagnostic attempts. After successful SuSiE signal construction and aggregation, coloc_best.tsv.gz contains 3,192 nonredundant xQTL-feature × GWAS-trait × ancestry comparisons, one row for the signal-pair with the highest PP4 in each comparison cell.

Headline result. 194 comparisons are called colocalized by the supplied coloc_call flag, spanning 66 primary gene IDs, 139 molecular phenotypes, and 15 GWAS traits. Calls divide into 92 EAS and 102 EUR comparisons. Increasing the posterior threshold leaves 167 PP4 ≥ 0.80, 115 PP4 ≥ 0.90, and 87 PP4 ≥ 0.95 comparisons.

The colocBoost aggregate adds 564 total colocalization-confidence-set (CoS) rows. 12 CoS events contain both placental xQTL and GWAS outcomes, representing 22 ancestry-matched gene–trait pairs across 13 genes and 12 GWAS traits. Of those pairs, 7 are also called by pairwise SuSiE-coloc and 15 are colocBoost-only, emphasizing complementarity rather than interchangeability of the two methods.

The dominant regulatory layers are alternative polyadenylation, expression, and isoform expression. The strongest recurrent locus is the chromosome 17p13 region containing ELP5, CLDN7, and CTDNEP1, which colocalizes with birth-weight and early-childhood anthropometric traits through multiple placental molecular readouts. Other recurrent signals include MRPL43, PLCE1-AS1, SH3YL1, WSB1, TFPI, CTSB, and an IGF2-overlapping composite stability phenotype.

No exact (modality, molecular phenotype, GWAS trait) call is shared between EAS and EUR, and no primary gene–trait call is shared across both ancestries. This is a major interpretive constraint: the atlas identifies compelling ancestry-stratified shared-signal candidates, not a catalog of cross-ancestry replicated colocalizations.

5.2 Methods

5.2.1 What SuSiE-coloc asks

For a pair of association signals, the coloc posterior partitions evidence among five hypotheses:

  • H0: neither trait is associated in the locus;
  • H1: xQTL only;
  • H2: GWAS only;
  • H3: both traits are associated, but through distinct causal variants;
  • H4: both traits are associated and share a causal variant.

The quantity emphasized here is PP.H4.abf (PP4). In a SuSiE-based analysis, multiple signals can exist within each locus. Each xQTL credible set can therefore be compared with each GWAS credible set; the full CS-pair output is stored in coloc_results.tsv.gz.

5.2.2 What colocBoost adds

colocBoost is a multi-trait colocalization framework that jointly models many molecular/GWAS outcomes and can accommodate multiple causal variants within a region. Its primary output is a colocalization confidence set (CoS): a set of variants and a configuration of outcomes inferred to share a causal effect.

This report defines a mixed xQTL–GWAS event as a CoS whose colocalized_outcomes contains at least one placental xQTL outcome and at least one gwas: outcome. Gene–trait pairs are generated only within those mixed events.

The colocBoost metrics are deliberately not converted into a PP4-like threshold. In particular, purity is an LD property of the CoS (minimum absolute within-set correlation), not a posterior probability of colocalization. cos_npc summarizes normalized colocalization support for the event, while min_npc_outcome reports the weakest normalized support among outcomes in the configuration. top_variable_vcp is the top variant’s colocalization probability. These quantities complement rather than numerically replace SuSiE-coloc PP4.

5.2.3 Why headline counts use coloc_best.tsv.gz

The full aggregate has 4,314 CS-pair rows but only 3,192 unique xQTL-feature × trait × ancestry cells. Counting every CS pair as an independent biological result would inflate loci that contain multiple signals. Therefore:

  1. coloc_results.tsv.gz is used for signal-pair diagnostics;
  2. coloc_best.tsv.gz supplies one maximum-PP4 signal pair per comparison cell;
  3. all headline call counts and gene/trait summaries use coloc_best.tsv.gz.

The supplied call flag is exactly equivalent to PP4 ≥ 0.7 in this aggregate.

A matching lead variant (hit1 == hit2) is supportive but is not required for H4: two association signals can share a causal variant even when their marginal/credible-set lead variants differ because of LD and sampling variation.

5.2.4 Gene and trait annotation

Primary gene IDs are parsed from the leading Ensembl gene ID in each molecular phenotype. If gene_map_path exists, its symbols are used. Otherwise the report falls back to Ensembl IDs plus a small curated symbol map for the most recurrent loci.

JECS phenotype codes are deliberately preserved in trait labels because the aggregate files do not include a complete phenotype dictionary for every code. The report does not infer age or measurement source beyond what is explicitly encoded in the supplied IDs.

5.5.1 Multi-GWAS developmental hotspots

The clearest added value of colocBoost is that one event can directly contain several GWAS traits. In the supplied aggregate, SH3YL1 forms an EAS event that couples multiple placental molecular modalities with four JECS childhood-BMI outcomes. PLCE1-AS1 forms another EAS event connecting isoform/poly(A) regulation with five JECS BMI outcomes. These should be interpreted as shared developmental-growth regulatory configurations, not as an equivalent number of independent causal variants.

Other mixed events include FAP, C3orf38, FES, FUBP1, GCLM, MRPL18/TCP1, BCKDHB, RBIS, ARF4, and NPEPL1. The FES–maternal-birth-weight event is particularly notable because prior placental multi-omics independently prioritized FES as a birth-weight effector candidate.

5.11 H3 versus H4: shared versus distinct causal signals

For loci in which both traits clearly associate, PP3 and PP4 are direct competitors: PP3 favors distinct causal signals, whereas PP4 favors sharing. This is why a high GWAS association signal near a strong xQTL is not sufficient for mechanistic interpretation without colocalization.

Among called comparisons, 31 / 194 (16.0%) use the same lead variant for xQTL and GWAS. The majority do not, illustrating why lead-SNP matching is an inadequate substitute for LD-aware colocalization.

5.14 Literature context

The companion literature_colocalization.md reviews the strongest and most recurrent loci in the context of placental biology and previous growth GWAS / placental multi-omics work.

The revised literature review also places the multi-trait colocBoost events in context. The strongest new external anchor is FES, which appears in a maternal-birth-weight colocBoost event and was independently prioritized by prior placental multi-omics. The SH3YL1 and PLCE1-AS1 events are emphasized as multi-timepoint developmental-growth hotspots because one CoS directly contains several JECS GWAS outcomes.

The chromosome 17p13 locus is the clearest external validation point: previous placental multi-omics work independently identified CTDNEP1 as a plausible birth-weight effector at a locus where birth weight, placental DNA methylation, and placental gene expression showed colocalization. The present atlas extends that locus from total expression into multiple long-read RNA-processing phenotypes and additionally highlights ELP5 and CLDN7.

5.15 Interpretation and limitations

  1. Colocalization is not mediation. H4 supports a shared causal genetic signal; it does not establish that changing the placental molecular feature causes the GWAS phenotype.
  2. The molecular feature’s gene label is not proof of the effector gene. This is especially important in compact loci such as chr17p13.
  3. PP4 ≥0.70 is inclusive. Results above 0.90–0.95 are the strongest candidates; 0.70–0.80 calls should be viewed as suggestive.
  4. Repeated molecular features are correlated. Multiple editing sites, transcripts, polyA sites, or modalities at one locus can reflect the same underlying haplotype.
  5. No exact cross-ancestry replication is observed. Power, LD and feature detection differ by ancestry, so ancestry contrast is descriptive rather than proof of effect heterogeneity.
  6. External LD matters. The project pipeline uses ancestry-matched 1000 Genomes LD for the SuSiE-coloc stage; mismatch between the LD reference and the underlying xQTL/GWAS study populations can alter signal decomposition.
  7. Aggregate outputs do not contain all harmonization details. Allele alignment and effect-direction auditing belong in the pipeline QC, not in this descriptive aggregate report.
  8. colocBoost evidence metrics are not PP4. purity describes within-CoS LD coherence; cos_npc, min_npc_outcome, and variant VCP carry different meanings and should not be thresholded or narrated as if they were Bayesian PP4 values.
  9. Pairwise and multi-trait non-overlap is expected. A colocBoost-only event can arise because joint modeling borrows configuration information across several outcomes or resolves multiple effects differently; it is not automatically a contradiction of SuSiE-coloc.
  10. Trait availability is uneven. The catalog is conditional on the GWAS traits included in this run and should not be interpreted as a comprehensive survey of pregnancy or childhood phenotypes.
  11. JECS codes are preserved. Exact timepoint semantics should be taken from the original GWAS phenotype dictionary before manuscript-level naming.
