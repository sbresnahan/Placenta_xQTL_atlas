Placenta xQTL Atlas

4 Literature context: fine-mapped loci

Project: Placenta xQTL atlas
Revision: 2026-10-10, rebuilt against the newly supplied report_finemap.html, finemap_locus_summary.tsv, and finemap_credible_sets.tsv.gz.

Companion literature review to report_finemap.html. It is the fine-mapping counterpart to literature_xqtl_mapping.md, which reviews the strongest variant-level association signals from the xQTL mapping report.

4.0.1 1. Background and scope

The current fine-mapping run uses SuSHiE to resolve significant placental xQTL loci across nine molecular modalities: gene expression, isoform expression, isoform usage, splicing, intron retention, alternative transcription start sites, alternative polyadenylation, RNA editing, and transcript stability. The run report records 5,529 loci attempted, 5,269 successfully fine-mapped, and 4,290 loci with at least one credible set.

this review does not retain the literature review’s claim of 5,236 high-confidence genes. Recalculation from the supplied results gives 2,055 current loci whose best credible set has lead PIP ≥ 0.9, spanning 1,142 unique Ensembl genes. Of those high-confidence loci, 1,899 (92.4%) have a single-variant credible set. Thus the strongest conclusion of the new run is not the sheer size of the gene catalog but the high degree of variant-level resolution among loci that pass the PIP threshold.

The biological reading is also different from the literature review. The old fine-mapping review emphasized a splicing-heavy set of established pregnancy and imprinted genes. In the new run, many of those loci are absent or no longer reach PIP ≥ 0.9. Instead, the leading pattern is cross-modality recurrence: WSB1 is high-confidence in six modalities, while SCARB2 and OLAH are high-confidence in five. Established placental and pregnancy genes remain in the catalog — including CYP19A1, SIGLEC6, ERAP1, KISS1, LGALS14, PSG3, PSG9, PECAM1, and HSPD1 — but the conflict/imprinting lens is much smaller, with IGF2R and SGCE providing the clearest retained examples.

Existing numeric citations [N] below preserve the numbering of the original fine-mapping literature review where the same literature-supported claim is retained. Newly added references are labeled [A1]–[A18] and are listed in Section 9. The companion finemap_hits_evidence_table.csv was not included with the new fine-mapping upload, so the new references are intentionally not assigned permanent numeric indices yet.

4.0.2 2. Methods and result reconciliation

4.0.2.1 2.1 Current-run membership

The supplied finemap_credible_sets.tsv.gz contains 6,347 credible-set rows, matching the total displayed in the HTML report. However, 763 of those rows (534 phenotype IDs) do not occur in the newly supplied finemap_locus_summary.tsv. The 4,290 current loci with credible sets in the locus summary account for 5,584 credible sets.

To prevent carry-over results from a previous mapping/fine-mapping run from entering the literature interpretation, all gene rankings in this review use only credible sets whose (modality, phenotype_id) pair is present in the new locus summary. This filtering step should also be incorporated into the report generation code, or the credible-set file should be regenerated cleanly.

4.0.2.2 2.2 High-confidence definition

For each current (modality, phenotype_id) locus, the credible set with the highest lead_pip was selected. A locus is called high-confidence when that best credible set has lead_pip >= 0.9. Gene IDs were taken from the Ensembl prefix of phenotype_id. This gives:

  • 2,055 high-confidence loci
  • 1,142 unique genes
  • 1,899 / 2,055 (92.4%) single-variant credible sets
  • 405 genes high-confidence in at least two modalities
  • 79 genes in at least three modalities
  • 25 genes in at least four modalities
  • only 3 genes in at least five modalities: WSB1, SCARB2, and OLAH.

4.0.3 3. Cross-cutting patterns

4.0.3.1 3.1 Multi-modality recurrence replaces splicing dominance as the main signal

The earlier review identified WSB1 and GBP3 as the most recurrent genes and then interpreted much of the pregnancy/conflict catalog through splicing. The new run retains WSB1 but changes the ranking substantially. WSB1 now spans six modalities, followed by SCARB2 and OLAH at five. Twenty-two additional genes span four modalities. The recurrent genes are therefore distributed across expression, isoform-level traits, RNA editing, RNA stability, and alternative ends rather than being dominated by one RNA-processing mechanism.

WSB1 remains the clearest “genetics first, biology second” discovery. It has high-confidence signals in RNA editing, expression, intron retention, isoform expression, isoform usage, and stability. Its known biology — HIF-responsive ubiquitin regulation and functionally distinct splice isoforms [13,14] — makes the recurrence plausible in a hypoxia-responsive organ, but a direct placental role remains poorly characterized.

SCARB2 and OLAH now join WSB1 as the two other five-modality genes. SCARB2 is a lysosomal membrane protein with strong protein expression in syncytiotrophoblast and Hofbauer cells [A1], while OLAH has unusually direct pregnancy relevance: placental OLAH changes across gestation and is altered in preeclampsia and fetal growth restriction; OLAH knockdown changes IGF2, oxidative-stress, and apoptosis-related transcription [A2].

4.0.3.2 3.2 The high-confidence catalog is extremely well resolved

A single-variant credible set is the minimum possible 95% CS. More than 92% of current PIP ≥0.9 loci are single-variant, and all 20 deep-dive genes have at least one such locus. This makes fine-mapping useful for variant prioritization, but the statistical precision should not be confused with mechanistic proof: a one-variant CS identifies a strong causal-regulatory candidate, not the molecular route by which it acts.

4.0.3.3 3.3 Established placental genes remain, but the positive controls changed

The current high-confidence catalog still contains biologically reassuring placental genes, but not the exact set highlighted previously. CYP19A1, SIGLEC6, ERAP1, KISS1, LGALS14, PSG3, PSG9, PECAM1, HSPD1 and HSP90B1 all retain at least one single-variant PIP ≥0.9 signal. By contrast, FLT1, PAPPA, ACVR2A, HSD11B2, IGF2, PEG3, MEST and GNAS are not current high-confidence genes, and GCM1, HLA-C and MEG3 are present only below the 0.9 threshold.

The implication is important: the literature review’s statement that fine-mapping “recovered known pregnancy genes as positive controls” was too dependent on an older result set. The new run should be interpreted from the loci it actually resolves, not from biologically attractive loci that were present previously.

4.0.3.4 3.4 Imprinting remains visible, but it is no longer a dominant catalog-wide theme

The literature review highlighted six canonical imprinted genes and stated that 27 imprinted genes were present in the high-confidence set. That claim is not supported by the current files supplied here. Among the original six, only IGF2R remains PIP ≥0.9. A second clear current imprinted signal is SGCE, which has a single-variant isoform-expression credible set. SGCE is maternally imprinted [A17], is expressed across human gestation [A16], and together with IGF2R has been implicated in environmentally responsive placental imprinted-gene expression [A18].

Thus intragenomic conflict remains a useful biological lens, but it is now a focused secondary theme rather than one of the dominant statistical patterns.

4.0.4 4. Gene snapshots

4.0.4.1 4.1 Recurrent and placental-biology signals

WSB1 — 6 modalities; 7 high-confidence loci; max lead PIP 1.00; min CS = 1.
WSB1 is the most recurrent gene in the current fine-mapped catalog. The resolved modalities are RNA editing, expression, intron retention, isoform expression, isoform usage, and stability. WSB1 encodes the substrate-recognition component of an E3 ubiquitin ligase and participates in hypoxia signaling [13]. Its splice isoforms can have distinct effects on proliferation and apoptosis [14], making the breadth of RNA-level regulation biologically credible. Direct placental characterization remains limited, which keeps WSB1 among the atlas’s strongest novel candidates.

SCARB2 — 5 modalities; 8 high-confidence loci; max lead PIP 1.00; min CS = 1.
SCARB2/LIMP-2 is a lysosomal membrane receptor. Unlike WSB1, its placental presence is directly supported by tissue proteomics: SCARB2 is high in syncytiotrophoblast cell body and microvilli and in Hofbauer cells [A1]. Fine-mapping across RNA editing, alternative TSS, expression, isoform expression and isoform usage therefore nominates a lysosome/membrane-trafficking axis operating in both trophoblast and placental macrophage compartments. Mechanistic pregnancy literature is still sparse.

OLAH — 5 modalities; 6 high-confidence loci; max lead PIP 1.00; min CS = 1.
OLAH is the strongest newly validated pregnancy-relevant recurrent signal. A human placental study found that OLAH expression rises with gestation, is altered in preterm preeclampsia and fetal growth restriction, and responds to placental dysfunction; OLAH knockdown perturbed BAX, BCL2, IGF2 and NOX4 expression [A2]. The current fine-map spans RNA editing, alt-TSS, expression, isoform expression and stability, converting that disease-expression observation into a candidate genetically regulated placental pathway.

CTSB — 4 modalities; 27 high-confidence loci; max lead PIP 1.00; min CS = 1.
CTSB has the largest number of high-confidence loci among the selected genes, distributed across RNA editing, alt-polyA, expression and isoform expression. Cathepsin B protein is high in placental trophoblast [A3]; experimental trophoblast work places CTSB downstream of beta-catenin-dependent migration and invasion in a hypoxia/preeclampsia context [A4]. The striking number of resolved CTSB molecular phenotypes suggests that this locus is not a single expression QTL but a dense regulatory hotspot.

GPNMB — 4 modalities; 12 high-confidence loci; max lead PIP 1.00; min CS = 1.
GPNMB is resolved across alt-polyA, expression, isoform expression and isoform usage. Placental single-cell reference data show particularly strong expression in Hofbauer cells [A5], pointing to placental macrophages rather than trophoblast as the most likely cellular context. This is a useful example of why cell-type attribution should accompany bulk-placenta QTL interpretation.

FOLR1 — 4 modalities; 5 high-confidence loci; max lead PIP 0.99995; min CS = 1.
FOLR1 encodes folate receptor-alpha, a core component of maternal-fetal folate transfer. FR-alpha localizes to the apical syncytiotrophoblast [A7], and placental FOLR1 expression/methylation has been studied in preterm birth [A6]. The four-modality fine-map — alt-TSS, expression, isoform expression and isoform usage — identifies several genetically controlled points at which placental folate transport could vary among individuals.

GBP3 — 4 modalities; 7 high-confidence loci; max lead PIP 1.00; min CS = 1.
GBP3 remains a recurrent innate-immunity signal, although its current recurrence is four rather than six modalities: expression, isoform expression, isoform usage and stability. GBPs are interferon-inducible antimicrobial GTPases [15], and GBP3 can participate in caspase-4-dependent pyroptosis [16]. There is still little direct placental literature, so GBP3 remains a high-confidence but biologically under-characterized maternal-fetal-interface candidate.

HSP90B1 — 3 modalities; 4 high-confidence loci; max lead PIP 0.99976; min CS = 1.
HSP90B1/GRP94 is resolved at alternative polyadenylation, expression and isoform expression. Human Protein Atlas staining is high in both trophoblastic and decidual cells [A8]. The locus therefore adds genetically regulated endoplasmic-reticulum stress/chaperone biology to the same broad stress axis represented by HSPD1 below.

4.0.4.2 4.2 Pregnancy and trophoblast-function signals

CYP19A1 — 3 modalities; 6 high-confidence loci; max lead PIP 1.00; min CS = 1.
CYP19A1 remains a strong placental positive control, although the new run supports three modalities rather than five: alt-polyA, intron retention and isoform expression. Aromatase drives placental estrogen synthesis and has trophoblast-specific promoter biology [17]; placental CYP activity is altered in preeclampsia-related models [19]. Fine-mapping indicates that genetically regulated transcript architecture, not only total expression, is relevant at this canonical placental gene.

SIGLEC6 — 2 modalities; 6 high-confidence loci; max lead PIP 1.00; min CS = 1.
SIGLEC6 remains one of the most biologically specific placental hits. It is a placenta-enriched molecule and circulating biomarker candidate in preeclampsia [21], with mechanistic evidence connecting excess SIGLEC6 to impaired syncytialization and inflammatory polarization [22,23]. The current fine-map resolves RNA-editing and expression loci rather than the four modalities stated previously.

ERAP1 — 4 modalities; 10 high-confidence loci; max lead PIP 1.00; min CS = 1.
ERAP1 remains one of the most compelling pregnancy-disease loci in the catalog. It participates in MHC-I peptide processing and vasoactive peptide metabolism, has placental and preeclampsia evidence [28,29], and sits directly at the trophoblast-immune interface [30]. The current resolved modalities are RNA editing, intron retention, isoform expression and stability. This still supports unusually broad regulatory control, but the old expression/splicing claims should not be carried forward.

IL1R1 — 3 modalities; 7 high-confidence loci; max lead PIP 0.9986; min CS = 1.
IL1R1 remains high-confidence across RNA editing, expression and isoform expression. IL-1 receptor signaling is a well-established inflammatory route into parturition and infection-induced preterm labor [43,44], and IL1R1 variation has been associated with chorioamnionitis risk [45]. The current fine-map therefore supports genetically regulated IL-1 responsiveness without requiring a splicing mechanism.

KISS1 — 2 modalities; 2 high-confidence loci; max lead PIP ~1.00; min CS = 1.
KISS1 is resolved for expression and isoform expression. Kisspeptin is tightly linked to trophoblast invasion: placental KISS1 is altered in preeclampsia, and experimental overexpression suppresses trophoblast invasion [A9,A10]. The QTL therefore provides a direct genetic route into a pathway already implicated in defective placentation.

LGALS14 — 3 modalities; 4 high-confidence loci; max lead PIP 1.00; min CS = 1.
LGALS14 is a primate placenta-associated galectin resolved for expression, isoform expression and stability. Galectin-14 promotes trophoblast migration and invasion and is reduced in early pregnancy loss and preeclampsia [A11]. The combination of placental specificity, disease dysregulation and single-variant QTL resolution makes LGALS14 a particularly interpretable fine-mapped locus.

PSG3 — 3 modalities; 7 high-confidence loci; max lead PIP 1.00; min CS = 1.
PSG3 is resolved for intron retention, isoform expression and isoform usage. Pregnancy-specific glycoproteins are produced in large amounts by placental trophoblast; PSG3 is among the genes strongly induced during trophoblast differentiation [A12]. The multi-layer signal suggests genetic control over both PSG3 abundance and transcript architecture.

PSG9 — 2 modalities; 6 high-confidence loci; max lead PIP 1.00; min CS = 1.
PSG9 is resolved for intron retention and isoform expression. Recent work identifies an LTR8B/PSG9 regulatory program controlling syncytialization and links elevated PSG9 to preeclampsia [A13]. The fine-mapped locus therefore intersects a newly defined, placenta-evolved regulatory mechanism.

PECAM1 — 2 modalities; 17 high-confidence loci; max lead PIP 1.00; min CS = 1.
PECAM1 has a large number of resolved RNA-editing and isoform-usage loci. Its placental role should be framed carefully: PECAM1 is a strong endothelial marker, but direct placental-bed studies did not support trophoblast PECAM1 as a driver of invasion or preeclampsia [A14]. The atlas signal may therefore reflect placental vascular/endothelial regulation rather than a trophoblast mechanism.

HSPD1 — RNA editing; 1 high-confidence locus; lead PIP 0.994; CS = 1.
HSPD1/Hsp60 is a lower-recurrence but biologically strong stress candidate. Recent work shows aberrant Hsp60 lactylation in preeclamptic trophoblasts, promoting mitochondrial fission and apoptosis [A15]. A single-variant RNA-editing QTL provides a distinct post-transcriptional layer on this mitochondrial stress pathway.

4.0.4.3 4.3 Intragenomic conflict and imprinting

IGF2R — expression; 1 high-confidence locus; lead PIP 0.9992; CS = 1.
IGF2R is the only one of the literature review’s six canonical conflict genes that remains high-confidence in the new run. It antagonizes IGF2 signaling and is a classic parent-of-origin growth-regulation locus [49]. Its clean single-variant expression credible set remains a compelling molecular connection between cis regulation and placental growth conflict.

SGCE — isoform expression; 1 high-confidence locus; lead PIP ~1.00; CS = 1.
SGCE is a new conflict-lens replacement for the prior IGF2/MEG3/PEG3/MEST/GNAS set. SGCE is maternally imprinted [A17], is expressed throughout human gestation [A16], and appears among placental imprinted genes whose expression tracks maternal one-carbon nutritional status [A18]. The resolved isoform-expression QTL therefore provides a current-run example of genetic regulation operating within an imprinted placental domain.

4.0.5 6. Synthesis

The fine-mapping catalog supports three conclusions.

First, fine-mapping resolution is exceptionally sharp: more than 92% of PIP≥0.9 current loci have one-variant credible sets. This creates a strong variant-prioritization resource even where biological annotation is sparse.

Second, the strongest recurring biology is multi-layer RNA regulation rather than splicing alone. WSB1, SCARB2, OLAH, CTSB, GPNMB, FOLR1, GBP3 and ERAP1 recur across multiple molecular readouts, often mixing transcript abundance, isoform regulation, RNA editing, intron retention, stability, and alternative ends. This is precisely the kind of regulatory architecture that a multi-modality xQTL atlas can reveal but a standard eQTL study cannot.

Third, the new run retains a coherent set of placental and pregnancy positive controls — CYP19A1, SIGLEC6, KISS1, LGALS14, PSG3/PSG9, PECAM1, HSPD1 and ERAP1 — while simultaneously weakening the prior claim that the fine-mapped catalog is dominated by canonical pregnancy GWAS and imprinted genes. The scientifically stronger interpretation is therefore that the atlas recovers known placental pathways and highlights recurrent loci whose placental role is incompletely characterized.

4.0.6 7. Limitations

  • Current-file mismatch. The credible-set file contains 534 loci absent from the new locus summary. This review filters them out, but the report generation pipeline should be corrected so all fine-mapping products share the same run membership.
  • PIP threshold is a statistical filter. Lead PIP ≥0.9 and a one-variant credible set are strong causal-regulatory evidence, not proof of molecular mechanism.
  • Gene-level recurrence is not locus independence. A gene with many high-confidence molecular phenotypes can contain correlated features driven by the same underlying variant/haplotype.
  • Bulk placenta mixes cell types. GPNMB, SCARB2 and PECAM1 illustrate why trophoblast, Hofbauer-cell and endothelial signals must be distinguished using cell-type-resolved annotations.
  • Literature selection is illustrative. The 20 deep dives prioritize recurrence and placental interpretability; they are not the only biologically important genes among the 1,142 current high-confidence genes.
  • Citation integration is incomplete. The original finemap_hits_evidence_table.csv was not supplied with this review. Legacy numeric citation indices are retained where possible; new sources remain [A#] until that evidence table is provided.
